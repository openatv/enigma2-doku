First establish **whether audio is early or late**. Use a known recording or sync test with a clear visual/audio event. A problem on one service only may originate in that source.

1. Temporarily reduce unnecessary TV picture processing, which can delay video. Keep the TV mode consistent for comparisons.
2. Check the route: receiver → AVR and receiver → TV → ARC can have different delays.
3. Note existing receiver, player, TV and AVR delays. Adjust one place at a time.
4. Change the relevant control in small increments. **Positive audio delay** makes sound later; it worsens already-late audio. Negative values are not implemented equally by every playback path.
5. Recheck PCM, a supported Dolby track and service switching.

## Which delay?

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| General AC3 delay | Global delay for the corresponding compressed/Dolby path. Source range −1000 to +1000 ms, 5 ms steps. |
| General PCM delay | Global delay for decoded PCM, with the same source range. |
| General BT audio delay | Additional driver-dependent Bluetooth path where available. |
| Service/player-specific delay | Provided by an installed audio-sync extension or player; can combine with global settings. |
| Passthrough handling delay AC3/AC3+ | Stabilisation time during format/service changes on certain platforms. **Not a substitute for ongoing lip-sync correction.** |
| CEC send/repeat interval | Timing between control messages, unrelated to audio/video synchronisation. |

An optional **AudioSync** plugin can provide finer global or per-service adjustments. Its menu depends on the installed version; it is not required for the two native general delay controls. Account for saved per-service corrections before changing a global value.

If the offset keeps growing during playback, investigate timestamps, stream, player and buffering. A fixed millisecond correction cannot permanently compensate for continuing drift. [Player troubleshooting](../../wiedergabe/formate/).
