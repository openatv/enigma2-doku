**A suitable hybrid tuner can use cable and aerial reception alternately.** On the SPAUN TAR 5, aerial voltage selects the input connected to the tuner: **0 V → CATV/cable, 5 V → DVB-T/aerial**. This is an RF relay, not another tuner or a satellite DiSEqC switch.

<figure><Image src={spaun} alt="Operator-supplied SPAUN TAR 5 photo showing Tuner/TV, DVB-T 5 V and CATV TV connectors" width={612} /><figcaption>Original photograph of the supplied TAR 5. Follow the label and datasheet of the actual hardware revision.</figcaption></figure>

## Connect the cables

1. Cable TV wall outlet to the TAR 5 **CATV / TV** socket.
2. Terrestrial aerial to **DVB-T / 5 V**.
3. TAR 5 **Tuner / TV** connector to the receiver’s shared **DVB-C/T2 input**.
4. Connect HDMI to the television as usual. Never connect a 13/18 V satellite output to this arrangement.

<Wiring id="spaun-5v" language="en" alt="SPAUN TAR 5 at 5 V: the aerial is connected to the hybrid tuner and cable TV is inactive" caption="For a terrestrial service, the receiver applies the configured 5 V supply. The relay selects the aerial branch." />

## Configure OpenATV

Open **Menu → Setup → Tuners, Scanning & Reception → Tuner Configuration → shared C/T2 tuner**. On supported hardware, configure both reception types: DVB-C with provider/scan parameters and DVB-T with country/region. **Enable 5 V for active antenna in the DVB-T section.** Here the supply also controls the relay.

Run cable and terrestrial scans separately. Add one service of each type to a test bouquet and switch between them. The driver must change aerial voltage as the reception type changes. If the receiver supports only one permanently selected mode, or lacks a controllable 5 V output, the relay alone cannot make this example work.

## Passive aerials and supply current

The TAR 5 still requires 5 V for switching when the aerial itself needs no power. For a **non-DC-compatible aerial**, SPAUN specifies a DC blocker in the **DVB-T aerial branch**. Place it between aerial and relay so that the receiver-to-relay control path still carries 5 V. Do not indiscriminately insert a blocker between receiver and TAR 5.

The photograph specifies 5 V, 35 mA and 5–862 MHz. Different documents/revisions list different DC-pass limits, so **check the limits of the actual relay and receiver** instead of applying a higher catalogue figure to the photographed device. The relay and any active aerial share the supply load. Its frequency range must cover the cable channels in use; this revision does not promise coverage above 862 MHz.

## Recordings remain tied to the reception type

A DVB-C recording occupies the shared tuner. Watching or recording DVB-T2 at the same time would change the input source and is not possible with this one frontend. Having both channel lists configured does not change this. [Multiple services within one multiplex](../single-tuner/) are a separate case.
